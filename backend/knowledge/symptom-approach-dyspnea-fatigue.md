# Approach to Common Presenting Symptoms: Dyspnoea, Fatigue, Dizziness

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines.

## Acute dyspnoea

Cardiac: acute heart failure, acute coronary syndrome, arrhythmia (atrial fibrillation), tamponade. Pulmonary: asthma or COPD exacerbation, pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, pneumothorax, pleural effusion. Other: anaemia, metabolic acidosis (e.g. DKA), sepsis, anaphylaxis and anxiety. Red flags: SpO₂ below 92%, respiratory rate above 24/min, inability to speak in sentences, chest pain, hypotension and altered consciousness. Useful early tests: pulse oximetry, ECG, chest radiograph, natriuretic peptide, troponin, D-dimer when PE probability is low/intermediate, full blood count, and blood gas.

## Fatigue

Consider anaemia (iron deficiency, B12/folate), hypothyroidism, uncontrolled diabetes or hyperglycaemia, chronic kidney disease, heart failure, depression, sleep disorders (obstructive sleep apnoea), medication side effects (beta-blockers, sedatives), infection, electrolyte disturbance (hyponatraemia, hypercalcaemia) and malignancy. Weight loss, night sweats, fever, or bleeding are red flags. Initial tests: full blood count, ferritin, TSH, glucose/HbA1c, renal and liver function, electrolytes and calcium.

## Dizziness and light-headedness

Pre-syncope: orthostatic hypotension (volume depletion, bleeding, antihypertensives, diuretics), arrhythmia (bradycardia, tachyarrhythmia), structural heart disease, and anaemia. Vertigo: peripheral (BPPV, vestibular neuritis) versus central (posterior circulation stroke; red flags include new headache, inability to walk, diplopia, dysarthria and focal neurology). Check orthostatic vital signs, ECG, haemoglobin and glucose. Melaena with dizziness and tachycardia suggests gastrointestinal bleeding.

## Palpitations and muscle weakness

Palpitations: atrial fibrillation and other arrhythmias, hyperthyroidism, anaemia, hypoglycaemia, electrolyte abnormalities (potassium, magnesium) and stimulant use. Generalised muscle weakness with palpitations or bradycardia should prompt urgent potassium measurement and ECG to exclude hyperkalaemia, especially in CKD or with RAAS inhibitors, potassium-sparing diuretics or NSAIDs.
