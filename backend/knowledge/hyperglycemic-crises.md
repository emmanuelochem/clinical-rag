# Hyperglycaemic Crises: DKA and HHS

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (e.g. ADA/EASD/JBDS consensus 2024).

## Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)

Diagnostic criteria: glucose of 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) or more or known diabetes, ketonaemia (beta-hydroxybutyrate 3.0 mmol/L or more) or significant ketonuria, and metabolic acidosis (pH below 7.3 and/or bicarbonate below 18 mmol/L). Euglycaemic DKA can occur with SGLT2 inhibitors, where glucose may be near normal; check ketones in any unwell patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor.

## Hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state (HHS)

Typically in type 2 diabetes: glucose of 600 mg/dL (33.3 mmol/L) or more, effective serum osmolality above 300 mOsm/kg, marked dehydration, and absence of significant ketoacidosis. Onset is over days with polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss and progressive confusion.

## Symptoms of hyperglycaemia

Polyuria, polydipsia, blurred vision, fatigue, weight loss and recurrent infections. Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain and Kussmaul breathing suggest ketoacidosis.

## Evaluation

Capillary and venous glucose, blood ketones, venous blood gas, electrolytes (sodium corrected for glucose; potassium before insulin), creatinine, osmolality, full blood count, and a search for precipitants (infection, myocardial infarction, missed insulin, new diabetes, drugs such as glucocorticoids).

## Management principles

Fluid resuscitation, intravenous insulin per protocol once potassium is 3.5 mmol/L or more, potassium replacement, frequent monitoring, and treatment of the precipitant.
