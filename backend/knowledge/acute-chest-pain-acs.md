# Acute Chest Pain and Acute Coronary Syndrome

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (e.g. ACC/AHA, ESC).

## Can't-miss causes of acute chest pain

Acute coronary syndrome (STEMI, NSTEMI, unstable angina), aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism, tension pneumothorax, pericardial tamponade and oesophageal rupture are the life-threatening causes that must be considered first. Common non-life-threatening causes include gastro-oesophageal reflux, musculoskeletal pain, pericarditis, pneumonia/pleurisy and anxiety.

## Features suggesting acute coronary syndrome

Pressure-like or squeezing retrosternal pain, radiation to one or both arms, jaw or neck, pain on exertion, associated diaphoresis, nausea or dyspnoea, and known coronary risk factors (diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, smoking, chronic kidney disease, family history) increase the likelihood of ACS. Women, older adults and people with diabetes more often present atypically with dyspnoea, fatigue or epigastric discomfort.

## Initial evaluation

- 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of arrival; repeat if symptoms recur or the first ECG is non-diagnostic.
- High-sensitivity cardiac troponin at presentation and repeated per a validated 0/1-hour or 0/2-hour algorithm. A rise and/or fall with at least one value above the 99th percentile upper reference limit indicates myocardial injury; with ischaemic symptoms or ECG changes this supports myocardial infarction.
- Troponin can also be elevated in heart failure, pulmonary embolism, sepsis, myocarditis, and chronic kidney disease; interpret trends in context.
- Risk stratification with HEART, TIMI or GRACE scores.
- Chest radiograph; consider CT aortography if dissection is suspected (tearing pain radiating to the back, pulse or blood pressure differential, new aortic regurgitation murmur).

## Early management principles (NSTE-ACS)

Aspirin unless contraindicated, antithrombotic therapy per local protocol, nitrates for ongoing ischaemic pain (avoid with hypotension or recent phosphodiesterase inhibitor use), oxygen only if SpO₂ is below 90%, high-intensity statin, and early cardiology involvement for invasive strategy depending on risk. STEMI requires immediate reperfusion pathway activation.
