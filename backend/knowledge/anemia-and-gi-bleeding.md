# Anaemia, Iron Deficiency and Gastrointestinal Bleeding

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (e.g. BSG, ACG).

## Anaemia classification by MCV

- Microcytic (MCV below 80 fL): iron deficiency, thalassaemia trait, anaemia of chronic disease (sometimes), sideroblastic anaemia.
- Normocytic: acute blood loss, chronic kidney disease, anaemia of chronic disease, haemolysis, marrow failure.
- Macrocytic (MCV above 100 fL): B12 or folate deficiency, alcohol, liver disease, hypothyroidism, myelodysplasia, drugs such as methotrexate.

## Iron deficiency

Ferritin below 30 ng/mL is diagnostic in most adults; ferritin is an acute-phase reactant and may be falsely normal in inflammation. In men and post-menopausal women, unexplained iron deficiency anaemia warrants evaluation of the gastrointestinal tract (bidirectional endoscopy) and coeliac serology. Long-term proton pump inhibitor use may reduce iron absorption.

## Symptoms

Fatigue, exertional dyspnoea, palpitations, light-headedness, reduced exercise tolerance, pallor; angina or heart failure in those with cardiac disease.

## Upper gastrointestinal bleeding

Haematemesis, coffee-ground vomitus or melaena (black, tarry stools). Causes include peptic ulcer disease (NSAIDs, aspirin, Helicobacter pylori), oesophagitis, varices and malignancy. Anticoagulants and antiplatelet agents increase bleeding risk and severity. Signs of instability: tachycardia, hypotension, postural symptoms, syncope. A blood urea nitrogen rise disproportionate to creatinine supports an upper GI source.

## Assessment and initial management principles

Haemodynamic assessment and resuscitation, full blood count, coagulation, group and crossmatch, renal function and urea. Glasgow-Blatchford score for risk stratification (score 0–1 may be suitable for outpatient management). Restrictive transfusion threshold (typically haemoglobin 7 g/dL; 8 g/dL with cardiovascular disease). Review and hold anticoagulants where appropriate with specialist input. Endoscopy within 24 hours for most upper GI bleeds.
