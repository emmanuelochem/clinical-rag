# Acute Decompensated Heart Failure

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (e.g. ESC 2021/2023, AHA/ACC/HFSA 2022).

## Presentation

Worsening exertional dyspnoea, orthopnoea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea, peripheral oedema, rapid weight gain (for example more than 2 kg in 3 days), fatigue and reduced exercise tolerance. Examination may show raised jugular venous pressure, bibasal crackles, a third heart sound, and pitting oedema.

## Common precipitants

Non-adherence to medication or fluid/salt restriction, acute coronary syndrome, arrhythmia (especially atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response), uncontrolled hypertension, infection, worsening renal function, and drugs that cause fluid retention (NSAIDs, thiazolidinediones, non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers in HFrEF).

## Investigations

- Natriuretic peptides: BNP below 100 pg/mL or NT-proBNP below 300 pg/mL makes acute heart failure unlikely. Values are higher with age and renal impairment and lower with obesity.
- ECG, chest radiograph (pulmonary congestion, effusions, cardiomegaly), troponin, renal function and electrolytes, full blood count, and echocardiography.
- Lung ultrasound (B-lines) can support pulmonary congestion.

## Management principles

Intravenous loop diuretics for congestion, with monitoring of urine output, weight, renal function and potassium. Oxygen when SpO₂ is below 90%. Non-invasive ventilation for respiratory distress with pulmonary oedema. Identify and treat precipitants. Continue guideline-directed medical therapy where possible (beta-blocker, ARNI/ACE inhibitor/ARB, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, SGLT2 inhibitor), adjusting for hypotension, hyperkalaemia or acute kidney injury.
