# Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease and Exacerbations

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (e.g. GOLD 2025).

## Diagnosis

Persistent respiratory symptoms (dyspnoea, chronic cough, sputum) with a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC below 0.70 on spirometry, usually with a history of tobacco smoke or other exposures.

## Exacerbations

An event with increased dyspnoea and/or cough and sputum worsening over 14 days or less. Common triggers are respiratory viral infections and bacterial infection. Increased sputum purulence suggests a bacterial cause. Differentials include pneumonia, acute heart failure, pulmonary embolism, pneumothorax and arrhythmia.

## Assessment

Pulse oximetry and arterial blood gas when SpO₂ is low or hypercapnia suspected; chest radiograph; full blood count and CRP; ECG and troponin/natriuretic peptide if cardiac causes are possible. Target SpO₂ 88–92% in patients at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure.

## Management principles

Short-acting bronchodilators, a short course of systemic corticosteroids (e.g. prednisone 40 mg for 5 days), antibiotics when sputum purulence is increased or ventilatory support is needed, controlled oxygen, and non-invasive ventilation for acute hypercapnic respiratory acidosis. Long-term: long-acting bronchodilators (LAMA/LABA), inhaled corticosteroid when eosinophils are high with frequent exacerbations, pulmonary rehabilitation, vaccination and smoking cessation.
