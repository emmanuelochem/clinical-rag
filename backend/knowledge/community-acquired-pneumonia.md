# Community-Acquired Pneumonia

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (e.g. ATS/IDSA 2019, BTS, NICE).

## Clinical features

Fever, cough (often productive; rust-coloured sputum is classic for pneumococcal pneumonia), pleuritic chest pain, dyspnoea and tachypnoea. Older adults may present with confusion or falls without fever. Examination may show focal crackles, bronchial breathing or dullness to percussion.

## Diagnosis

Chest radiograph showing a new infiltrate confirms the diagnosis in the right clinical context. Supporting findings include leukocytosis, elevated CRP (often above 100 mg/L in bacterial pneumonia) and procalcitonin. Blood and sputum cultures are recommended for severe disease. Consider influenza and SARS-CoV-2 testing when circulating.

## Severity assessment

CURB-65 assigns one point each for Confusion, Urea above 7 mmol/L, Respiratory rate of 30/min or more, low Blood pressure (systolic below 90 or diastolic 60 or below mmHg), and age 65 or older. A score of 0–1 suggests outpatient treatment, 2 hospital admission, and 3 or more severe pneumonia requiring consideration of higher-level care. Hypoxaemia (SpO₂ below 92%) warrants admission regardless of score.

## Differential diagnosis

Acute exacerbation of COPD or asthma, acute heart failure with pulmonary oedema, pulmonary embolism (with infarction), lung cancer with post-obstructive pneumonia, and tuberculosis.

## Treatment principles

Empirical antibiotics according to severity, local resistance patterns and allergy history (note penicillin allergy), started promptly. Assess for sepsis. Reassess at 48–72 hours.
