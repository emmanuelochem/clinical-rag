# Pulmonary Embolism

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (e.g. ESC 2019, ACCP/CHEST).

## Presentation

Dyspnoea (often sudden), pleuritic chest pain, cough, haemoptysis, syncope, tachycardia, tachypnoea and hypoxaemia. Signs of deep vein thrombosis (unilateral leg swelling, calf tenderness) support the diagnosis. Massive PE presents with hypotension or shock.

## Risk factors

Recent surgery or immobilisation, active cancer, prior venous thromboembolism, pregnancy or oestrogen therapy, long-haul travel, thrombophilia, and heart failure.

## Diagnostic approach

Assess pre-test probability with Wells or revised Geneva score. In low-probability patients, the PERC rule can exclude PE without testing. D-dimer (age-adjusted cut-off over 50 years: age × 10 µg/L) for low or intermediate probability; CT pulmonary angiography when probability is high or D-dimer is positive. Troponin and BNP elevation and right ventricular dysfunction on echo or CT indicate higher risk.

## Management principles

Anticoagulation (DOAC for most haemodynamically stable patients without contraindications); systemic thrombolysis for high-risk PE with haemodynamic instability. Consider bleeding risk and renal function.
