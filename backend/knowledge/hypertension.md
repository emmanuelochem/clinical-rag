# Hypertension

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (e.g. ACC/AHA 2017, ESC 2024).

## Classification (ACC/AHA)

Normal below 120/80 mmHg; elevated 120–129/below 80; stage 1 130–139/80–89; stage 2 140/90 or higher. Confirm with out-of-office measurement (home or ambulatory monitoring) to detect white-coat or masked hypertension.

## Hypertensive urgency and emergency

Severe hypertension (typically 180/120 mmHg or higher) with acute target-organ damage is a hypertensive emergency: hypertensive encephalopathy, stroke, acute coronary syndrome, acute heart failure with pulmonary oedema, aortic dissection, acute kidney injury or eclampsia. Without acute organ damage, it is managed by gradual reduction with oral agents.

## Treatment principles

Lifestyle measures (sodium restriction, weight loss, physical activity, alcohol moderation). First-line drug classes: ACE inhibitors or ARBs, dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers and thiazide-like diuretics. Target below 130/80 mmHg for most adults. ACE inhibitors/ARBs are preferred with albuminuric CKD or diabetes; monitor potassium and creatinine 1–2 weeks after starting or increasing dose. Resistant hypertension (uncontrolled on three agents including a diuretic) warrants evaluation for secondary causes such as primary aldosteronism and obstructive sleep apnoea.

## Symptoms

Usually asymptomatic. Headache, visual changes, chest pain, dyspnoea or neurological symptoms with very high blood pressure suggest target-organ damage.
