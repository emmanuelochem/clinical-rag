# Sepsis Recognition and Initial Management

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021).

## Definition

Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection (Sepsis-3), identified by an acute increase in SOFA score of 2 or more. Septic shock: vasopressors needed to maintain mean arterial pressure of 65 mmHg or more and lactate above 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation.

## Screening

qSOFA (respiratory rate of 22/min or more, altered mentation, systolic blood pressure of 100 mmHg or less) identifies patients at higher risk of poor outcome but is not recommended as a single screening tool. NEWS2 of 5 or more, or SIRS criteria, can prompt sepsis evaluation. Older and immunosuppressed patients may lack fever.

## Initial management (first hour)

Measure lactate, obtain blood cultures before antibiotics, give broad-spectrum antibiotics promptly (within 1 hour for probable sepsis or shock), give 30 mL/kg intravenous crystalloid for hypotension or lactate of 4 mmol/L or more, and start vasopressors if hypotensive during or after fluids. Identify and control the source (pneumonia, urinary tract, abdominal, skin/soft tissue, line-related).
