# Hyperkalaemia

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (e.g. UK Kidney Association 2023).

## Definition and severity

Serum potassium above 5.5 mmol/L (some laboratories use above 5.0). Mild 5.5–5.9, moderate 6.0–6.4, severe 6.5 mmol/L or more, or any level with ECG changes. Exclude pseudohyperkalaemia (haemolysed sample, high platelet or white cell counts).

## Causes

Reduced renal excretion (acute kidney injury, chronic kidney disease), drugs (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNI, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, potassium-sparing diuretics, NSAIDs, trimethoprim, heparin, calcineurin inhibitors), potassium supplements, tissue breakdown (rhabdomyolysis, tumour lysis, haemolysis), metabolic acidosis, insulin deficiency and hypoaldosteronism. NSAIDs combined with RAAS inhibitors in CKD are a common precipitant ("triple whammy" with diuretics).

## Clinical features

Often asymptomatic. Muscle weakness, paraesthesia, palpitations, bradycardia and arrhythmias. ECG changes: peaked T waves, PR prolongation, loss of P waves, QRS widening, sine-wave pattern, ventricular fibrillation or asystole.

## Emergency management principles

ECG and cardiac monitoring. Intravenous calcium to stabilise the myocardium if ECG changes or potassium is 6.5 mmol/L or more. Shift potassium into cells with insulin-glucose and nebulised salbutamol. Remove potassium with potassium binders, loop diuretics or dialysis. Stop contributing drugs and review once stable.
