# Chronic Kidney Disease

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (e.g. KDIGO 2024).

## Definition and staging

Abnormalities of kidney structure or function present for more than 3 months. Staged by eGFR (G1 90 or more; G2 60–89; G3a 45–59; G3b 30–44; G4 15–29; G5 below 15 mL/min/1.73m²) and albuminuria (A1 urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio below 30 mg/g; A2 30–300; A3 above 300). Risk of progression and cardiovascular events rises with lower eGFR and higher albuminuria.

## Monitoring

eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio at least annually, more often with higher risk. A sustained decline in eGFR of more than 5 mL/min/1.73m² per year, or a sustained 25% drop with a change in category, indicates rapid progression. Monitor potassium, bicarbonate, haemoglobin, calcium, phosphate and PTH in advanced stages.

## Management principles

Blood pressure control (ACE inhibitor or ARB for albuminuria), SGLT2 inhibitor for most patients with eGFR of 20 or more, statin therapy, glycaemic control in diabetes, and avoidance of nephrotoxins (NSAIDs, iodinated contrast where avoidable). Adjust renally cleared drug doses (metformin, DOACs, gabapentinoids, antibiotics). Refer to nephrology for eGFR below 30, ACR above 300 mg/g, rapid decline, refractory hypertension or persistent hyperkalaemia.

## Complications

Hyperkalaemia, metabolic acidosis, anaemia of CKD, mineral and bone disorder, fluid overload, and increased cardiovascular risk. Acute kidney injury superimposed on CKD is commonly triggered by volume depletion, sepsis, NSAIDs or RAAS blockade during acute illness.
