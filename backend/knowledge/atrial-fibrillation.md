# Atrial Fibrillation

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (e.g. ACC/AHA/HRS 2023, ESC 2024).

## Presentation

Palpitations, dyspnoea, fatigue, dizziness, reduced exercise tolerance, chest discomfort, or no symptoms. Irregularly irregular pulse. Diagnosis requires ECG documentation.

## Stroke prevention

Assess stroke risk with CHA₂DS₂-VASc (congestive heart failure, hypertension, age 75 or older [2 points], diabetes, prior stroke/TIA/thromboembolism [2 points], vascular disease, age 65–74, female sex). Oral anticoagulation, preferably a DOAC such as apixaban, is recommended for most patients with elevated risk. Assess and address modifiable bleeding risk factors; DOAC doses depend on renal function, age and weight.

## Rate and rhythm control

Rate control with beta-blockers or non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers (avoid the latter in HFrEF); a lenient resting heart rate target below 110 bpm is acceptable for many patients. Rhythm control should be considered early, particularly in symptomatic patients and those with heart failure.

## Complications and associations

Stroke and systemic embolism, tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy and heart failure. Precipitants and associations include hypertension, heart failure, valvular disease, hyperthyroidism, sepsis, alcohol, obstructive sleep apnoea and pulmonary embolism.
