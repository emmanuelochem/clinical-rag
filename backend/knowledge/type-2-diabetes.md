# Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus: Monitoring and Management

> Demo reference summary authored for this application. Verify against current guidelines (e.g. ADA Standards of Care).

## Diagnosis

HbA1c of 6.5% or more, fasting plasma glucose of 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) or more, 2-hour glucose of 200 mg/dL or more on OGTT, or random glucose of 200 mg/dL or more with classic symptoms. Prediabetes: HbA1c 5.7–6.4%.

## Glycaemic targets

An HbA1c goal below 7% is appropriate for many non-pregnant adults; less stringent targets (below 8%) for older adults with frailty, limited life expectancy or hypoglycaemia risk; tighter targets where safely achievable. HbA1c reflects average glucose over about three months (estimated average glucose in mg/dL ≈ 28.7 × HbA1c − 46.7). Monitor HbA1c every 3 months when not at goal or after therapy changes, and every 6 months when stable.

## Pharmacological therapy

Metformin is first-line for most patients (contraindicated with eGFR below 30; do not initiate with eGFR 30–45). For patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure or chronic kidney disease, add an SGLT2 inhibitor and/or GLP-1 receptor agonist with proven benefit, independent of HbA1c. Rising HbA1c despite adherence indicates the need to intensify therapy.

## Complication screening

Annual urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and eGFR; annual dilated eye examination; annual comprehensive foot examination; lipid management with statin therapy for most adults aged 40–75; blood pressure target below 130/80 mmHg.
